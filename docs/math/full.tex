\chapter{Multivariate heteroskedastic BART}
\label{ch:full}

This chapter derives, from scratch, every step of the BART sampler for the
most general model treated in this document:
\begin{itemize}
	\item each outcome $\vy_i$ is a $k$-vector, whose components are each
	      either continuous or binary; binary components are modelled with a
	      probit link, through a latent Gaussian outcome;
	\item each leaf parameter $\vmu_\ell$ is a $k$-vector, with a centred
	      multivariate-Gaussian prior of precision $\mLamm$;
	\item the noise on the (latent) outcome has precision $\mA_i \mLam \mA_i$
	      where $\mA_i = \diag\va_i$ is a fixed (known) diagonal matrix of
	      nonnegative datapoint-specific precision scales, and $\mLam$ is a
	      global $k \times k$ error precision;
	\item $\mLam$ has a Wishart conjugate prior, or, when it is restricted to
	      be diagonal, independent gamma priors on its entries, with the
	      entries of the binary components fixed;
	\item trees follow the Chipman--George--McCulloch (CGM) prior and are
	      updated by GROW/PRUNE moves only;
	\item the split variable of each decision rule is drawn from a
	      probability vector $\vs$ with a Dirichlet prior, whose concentration
	      has a Beta hyperprior (the variable-selection prior of Linero).
\end{itemize}
Working in the multivariate setting it is more convenient to deal with
precision matrices than with covariance matrices, so throughout this chapter
$\mLam$ and $\mLamm$ are the primary parameters: all Gaussian distributions
are written in precision form $\N_k(\cdot, \mLam^{-1})$ and the conjugate
prior on $\mLam$ is a Wishart (rather than an inverse-Wishart on the
covariance $\mLam^{-1}$). Using precision scales $\va_i$ in place of weights
also relaxes positivity: $a_{i,j} = 0$ is admissible and corresponds to a
missing $j$-th component of $\vy_i$ (infinite noise on that coordinate).
Following bartz, when some component can be missing on its own or is binary,
$\mLam$ is restricted to be diagonal (\S\ref{sec:mv-model}).

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Notation and data}
\label{sec:mv-notation}

We observe $n$ pairs $(\vx_i, \vy_i)_{i=1}^n$ with $\vx_i \in \R^p$ and
$\vy_i \in \R^k$. Each component $j$ of the outcome has a fixed type,
continuous or binary; let $\setB \subseteq \{1, \dots, k\}$ be the set of
binary components and $\setC$ its complement, the continuous ones, so that
$y_{i,j} \in \R$ for $j \in \setC$ and $y_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\}$ for
$j \in \setB$. Stack the responses as rows into the matrix
$Y \in \R^{n \times k}$ and the covariates row-by-row into the design matrix
$X = (\vx_1, \dots, \vx_n)^\top \in \R^{n \times p}$. In addition, each
datapoint comes with a vector of nonnegative precision scales
$\va_i \in \R^k_{\geq 0}$, treated as fixed and known (part of the
observation); we collect them in $\mA_i = \diag\va_i$.

A \emph{decision tree} $T$ is a binary tree whose internal nodes carry a
splitting rule of the form ``$x_{v} < c$'', for some variable index
$v \in \{1, \dots, p\}$ and cutpoint $c$. Given $T$ and a covariate $\vx$, the
tree assigns $\vx$ to a unique leaf $\ell(\vx; T)$ by following the rules from
the root. We write $L(T)$ for the set of leaves of $T$ and
$|L(T)|$ for their number. To each tree we attach a vector-valued leaf array
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-leaf-array}
	M = \{ \vmu_\ell : \ell \in L(T) \} \subset \R^k,
\end{equation}
and define the corresponding $k$-dimensional regression function
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-g}
	g(\vx; T, M) = \sum_{\ell \in L(T)} \vmu_\ell \Ind[\ell(\vx; T) = \ell]
	\in \R^k.
\end{equation}
A BART model uses a sum of $m$ such trees,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-sum-of-trees}
	f(\vx) = \sum_{t=1}^m g(\vx; T_t, M_t) \in \R^k.
\end{equation}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The generative model}
\label{sec:mv-model}

\subsection{Conditional outcome distribution}

Each binary component is modelled through a latent continuous outcome
$z_{i,j}$ that is observed only through its sign, the probit data
augmentation of Albert and Chib (1993). Define the \emph{working outcome}
$\vyt_i \in \R^k$ by
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-working}
	\tilde y_{i,j} =
	\begin{cases}
		y_{i,j} & j \in \setC, \\
		z_{i,j} & j \in \setB,
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
so that $\vyt_i = \vy_i$ when all components are continuous. Conditional on
the trees and the noise precision, the working outcomes are
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-lik}
	\vyt_i \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam \stackrel{\text{ind}}{\sim}
	\N_k\left(f(\vx_i), (\mA_i \mLam \mA_i)^{-1}\right),
	\qquad
	y_{i,j} = \Ind[z_{i,j} > 0], \quad j \in \setB,
\end{equation}
for $i = 1, \dots, n$. Equivalently, in terms of rescaled residuals,
$\va_i \odot (\vyt_i - f(\vx_i)) \sim \N_k(\mathbf{0}, \mLam^{-1})$. We will
use this form repeatedly. For a binary component, since $\mLam$ is diagonal
with $\Lambda_{jj} = 1$ for $j \in \setB$ (\S\ref{sec:mv-Lam-prior}), the
link is
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-probit}
	P(y_{i,j} = 1 \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}) = \Phi\left(a_{i,j} f_j(\vx_i)\right),
	\qquad j \in \setB,
\end{equation}
with $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function: the precision scale
acts as the inverse scale of the latent outcome.

Viewed as a function of the parameters $(T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam)$ and of the
latent outcomes with $Y$ fixed, the product over $i$ of \eqref{eq:mv-lik}
(including the indicators) is the \emph{likelihood} of the model. We will use
the word ``likelihood'' only in this strict sense, and only in contexts where
the parameters (or some marginal thereof) are the free variables.

\paragraph{Missing components.}

A component $y_{i,j}$ with $a_{i,j} = 0$ has infinite noise, i.e., it is
missing: the information carried by $\vy_i$ is the marginal distribution of
its other components. Let
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-Oi}
	O_i \coloneqq \{j : a_{i,j} > 0\},
	\qquad
	k_i \coloneqq |O_i|,
\end{equation}
be the observed components of $\vy_i$ and their number, and $\bar O_i$ the
missing ones. Marginalising \eqref{eq:mv-lik} over $\vyt_{i,\bar O_i}$, the
covariance of $\vyt_{i,O_i}$ is the $O_i$ block of
$\mA_i^{-1}\mLam^{-1}\mA_i^{-1}$, so
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:mv-lik-obs}
	\vyt_{i,O_i} \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam & \stackrel{\text{ind}}{\sim}
	\N_{k_i}\left(f(\vx_i)_{O_i},
	\left(\mA_{i,O_i}\left[(\mLam^{-1})_{O_iO_i}\right]^{-1}\mA_{i,O_i}\right)^{-1}\right),
	\\
	\mA_{i,O_i}                              & \coloneqq \diag\va_{i,O_i},
	\notag
\end{align}
where $[(\mLam^{-1})_{O_iO_i}]^{-1} = \mLam_{O_iO_i} -
	\mLam_{O_i\bar O_i}\mLam_{\bar O_i\bar O_i}^{-1}\mLam_{\bar O_iO_i}$ is the
Schur complement of $\mLam_{\bar O_i\bar O_i}$ in $\mLam$. This does not
depend on the missing scales, so it is also the limit $a_{i,j} \to 0$,
$j \in \bar O_i$, of the complete model. The precision in
\eqref{eq:mv-lik-obs} depends on $\mLam$ and on the missingness pattern of
$i$ in a way that does not factor as $\mLam$ times a data-only matrix, which
would break the sufficient statistics of \S\ref{sec:mv-partial-resid} and the
conjugacy of \S\ref{sec:mv-Sigma}. For this reason, following bartz,
\emph{whenever some component can be missing while others of the same
	datapoint are observed we restrict $\mLam$ to be diagonal}; the binary
components require the same restriction, for a different reason explained in
\S\ref{sec:mv-Lam-prior}. Missingness of whole datapoints ($O_i = \emptyset$
or $O_i = \{1, \dots, k\}$ for every $i$) needs no restriction: such an $i$
merely drops out of every sum over datapoints, so all that follows holds with
$n$ read as the number of datapoints that are present. Under the
restriction,
$[(\mLam^{-1})_{O_iO_i}]^{-1} = \mLam_{O_iO_i}$, and \eqref{eq:mv-lik-obs}
becomes the Gaussian whose precision is $\mA_i\mLam\mA_i$ restricted to the
observed rows and columns,
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:mv-lik-diag}
	\vyt_{i,O_i} \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam & \stackrel{\text{ind}}{\sim}
	\N_{k_i}\left(f(\vx_i)_{O_i},
	[\mA_i \mLam \mA_i]_{O_iO_i}^{-1}\right),
	\\
	[\va_i \odot (\vyt_i - f(\vx_i))]_{O_i}  & \sim
	\N_{k_i}(\mathbf{0}, \mLam_{O_iO_i}^{-1}).
	\notag
\end{align}
The missing entries of $\vyt_i$ may be set to any value, since they are
always multiplied by $a_{i,j} = 0$; in particular the latent outcome of a
missing binary component is unconstrained by the data and drops out of the
model. In what follows, formulas written with the full $k \times k$
precision $\mA_i\mLam\mA_i$, whose missing rows and columns vanish, are to be
read as \eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag}.

Many formulas written without taking into account that $a$ can be 0 continue to
be correct in the missing data case, with the null components of $\mathbf a_i$
implicitly deleting the terms that ought to disappear (again, only under the
restriction that $\mLam$ be diagonal). The implementation takes advantage of
this to simplify the code. In some places this does not hold, and we will point
it out.

\subsection{Tree-structure prior}
\label{sec:mv-tree-prior}

A priori, each tree $T_t$ is drawn independently from the CGM process. Working
node-by-node from the root, a node at depth $d \ge 0$ (with the root at depth
$0$) is, conditionally on existing, internal (i.e., nonterminal) with
probability
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:pnt}
	p_{\mathrm{NT}}(d) = \alpha (1 + d)^{-\beta},
	\qquad \alpha \in (0,1),\ \beta > 0,
\end{equation}
and otherwise a leaf. The implementation truncates the recursion at a maximum
depth $d_{\max}$, enforced by setting $p_{\mathrm{NT}}(d_{\max}) = 0$ (in
\texttt{bartz/mcmcstep/\_state.py}, \texttt{make\_p\_nonterminal} evaluates
\eqref{eq:pnt} at depths $0, \dots, d_{\max} - 1$ and \texttt{init} appends
the final $0$ through \texttt{parse\_p\_nonterminal}).

Conditionally on a node $\eta$ being internal, its splitting rule is drawn
from
\begin{itemize}
	\item the distribution on the variables $v$ that still admit at least
	      one valid cutpoint at $\eta$ (the \emph{eligible} set $E(\eta)$)
	      with probabilities proportional to a global probability vector
	      $\vs = (s_1, \dots, s_p)$,
	      \begin{equation}
		      \label{eq:mv-var-prior}
		      p(v \mid \eta, \vs) =
		      \begin{cases}
			      \dfrac{s_v}{e_\eta} & v \in E(\eta), \\[1ex]
			      0                   & \text{otherwise},
		      \end{cases}
		      \qquad
		      e_\eta \coloneqq \sum_{u \in E(\eta)} s_u,
	      \end{equation}
	      and
	\item a uniform distribution on the cutpoints $c$ that are compatible with
	      the ancestor splits along the path to the root.
\end{itemize}
Each predictor $v$ comes with a fixed list of allowed cutpoints, specified as
part of the model (separate from, though often derived by default from, the
values $X_{\cdot,v}$ observed in the training set). ``Compatible'' means
that the cutpoint lies strictly between the inherited lower and upper bounds
imposed by the ancestor splits on variable $v$ along the path to the root.
In \texttt{bartz} the allowed cutpoints are pre-binned and live in the
integer range $[1, \texttt{max\_split}_v]$ (see the
\texttt{bartz.prepcovars} module). We assume every variable has at least one
cutpoint globally, so $E(\eta)$ only shrinks because of ancestor splits.
bartz allows variables with none (\texttt{max\_split}$_v = 0$) by
renormalising $\vs$ over the other variables, which is what the rule prior
depends on; by the aggregation property of the Dirichlet, this leaves the
derivations of this chapter unchanged on the renormalised subvector.

We write $p(T_t \mid \vs)$ for the probability of the tree structure
(including the splitting rules) under this prior.

\subsection{Variable selection prior}
\label{sec:mv-sparse-prior}

The split-variable probabilities follow the sparsity-inducing prior of
Linero (2018),
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-s-prior}
	\vs \mid \theta \sim \Dir\left(\frac{\theta}{p}, \dots, \frac{\theta}{p}\right),
\end{equation}
shared by all trees. Small concentrations $\theta$ put most of the mass of
$\vs$ on a few variables. The concentration is either fixed or given the
hyperprior
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-theta-prior}
	\xi \coloneqq \frac{\theta}{\theta + \rho} \sim \Betad(a_\theta, b_\theta),
\end{equation}
with fixed $\rho > 0$ (by default $\rho = p$) and shapes
$a_\theta, b_\theta > 0$ (\texttt{a}, \texttt{b} in bartz). This chapter
covers the hyperprior; fixing $\theta$ amounts to skipping its update in
\S\ref{sec:mv-sparse}.

\subsection{Leaf prior}

Conditional on the tree, the leaf parameters are i.i.d.\ centred multivariate
Gaussian in precision form:
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-leaf-prior}
	\vmu_\ell \mid T_t \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}
	\N_k\left(\mathbf{0}, \mLamm^{-1}\right),
	\qquad \ell \in L(T_t).
\end{equation}
The precision $\mLamm$ is calibrated so that the prior on the sum of trees,
$f(\vx) \sim \N_k(\mathbf{0}, m \mLamm^{-1})$, has user-controlled
variability.

\subsection{Error precision prior}
\label{sec:mv-Lam-prior}

The global noise precision $\mLam$ takes one of two forms.

\paragraph{Dense.}
When all components are continuous ($\setB = \emptyset$) and missingness, if
any, is by whole datapoint, $\mLam$ follows the conjugate Wishart prior
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-iw-prior}
	\mLam \sim \W(\nu, \Psi),
\end{equation}
parametrised so that $\Psi \in \R^{k \times k}$ is the prior inverse-scale
(rate) matrix and $\nu > k - 1$ the degrees of freedom; the prior density is
proportional to $|\mLam|^{(\nu - k - 1)/2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}
	\tr(\Psi \mLam)\right)$.

\paragraph{Diagonal.}
When some component is binary or can be missing on its own,
$\mLam = \diag(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k)$ with independent entries,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-gamma-prior}
	\lambda_j \sim
	\begin{cases}
		\Gamma\left(\dfrac{\nu_j}{2}, \dfrac{\psi_j}{2}\right) & j \in \setC, \\[2ex]
		\delta_1                                               & j \in \setB,
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma(\alpha, \beta)$ the shape--rate gamma and $\delta_1$ the point
mass at $1$. The entries of the binary components are fixed because the
indicator in \eqref{eq:mv-lik} is invariant under a common rescaling of
$z_{i,j}$ and $f_j(\vx_i)$, so their scale is not identified; the unit choice
gives the probit link \eqref{eq:mv-probit}.

The two forms are calibrated to agree marginally: under \eqref{eq:mv-iw-prior}
each variance $(\mLam^{-1})_{jj}$ is a priori
$\IG((\nu - k + 1)/2, \Psi_{jj}/2)$, which is the prior of $1/\lambda_j$
under \eqref{eq:mv-gamma-prior} with
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-gamma-match}
	\nu_j = \nu - k + 1,
	\qquad
	\psi_j = \Psi_{jj}.
\end{equation}
For $k = 1$ and a continuous outcome the two forms coincide. This is the
calibration used by bartz, where the user sets the degrees of freedom
$\nu_j$ of the marginal variances (\texttt{sigma\_df}), whatever the form
of $\mLam$.

The binary components require the diagonal form for a reason unrelated to
missingness. With a dense $\mLam$, the latent outcomes of a datapoint would be
correlated across components: their update (\S\ref{sec:mv-z}) would become
a draw from a multivariate truncated normal, and the off-diagonal entries of
$\mLam$ involving binary components would need a constrained prior to be
identified, as in multivariate probit regression. The diagonal form
sidesteps both, and the components remain coupled only through the shared
trees.

\subsection{Bayesian network}
\label{sec:mv-bn}

Let $Z$ be the latent outcomes of the observed binary components,
$\{z_{i,j} : j \in \setB \cap O_i\}$; those of the missing ones are dropped
from the model along with the missing continuous components. Putting the
pieces together, the joint density factorises as
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:mv-joint}
	p(Y, Z, T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam, \vs, \theta)
	 & = p(\theta) p(\vs \mid \theta) p(\mLam)
	\prod_{t=1}^m p(T_t \mid \vs) p(M_t \mid T_t)
	\\
	 & \quad \cdot \prod_{i=1}^n p(\vyt_{i,O_i} \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam)
	\prod_{j \in \setB \cap O_i} p(y_{i,j} \mid z_{i,j}),
	\notag
\end{align}
with $p(M_t \mid T_t) = \prod_{\ell \in L(T_t)} \N_k(\vmu_\ell;
	\mathbf{0}, \mLamm^{-1})$, $p(\vyt_{i,O_i} \mid \cdots)$ the Gaussian of
\eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag} (or of \eqref{eq:mv-lik} in the dense case), and
$p(y_{i,j} \mid z_{i,j}) = \Ind[y_{i,j} = \Ind[z_{i,j} > 0]]$.

The precision scales $\va_i$ enter as fixed, observed inputs alongside
$\vx_i$. The edge from $\vyt_i$ to $\vy_i$ is the identity on the continuous
components and the sign on the binary ones.

\section{Single-tree decomposition and partial residuals}
\label{sec:mv-partial-resid}

Bayesian backfitting handles \eqref{eq:mv-sum-of-trees} by updating one tree
at a time, holding the others fixed. Define the \emph{partial residual} for
tree $t$ by removing every other tree's contribution:
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-partial-resid}
	\vr_i^{(t)} \coloneqq \vyt_i - \sum_{k \ne t} g(\vx_i; T_k, M_k)
	\in \R^k.
\end{equation}
With the latent outcomes held fixed at their current values, by
\eqref{eq:mv-lik},
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-partial-lik}
	\vr_i^{(t)} \mid T_t, M_t, \mLam \stackrel{\text{ind}}{\sim}
	\N_k\left(g(\vx_i;  T_t, M_t),  (\mA_i \mLam \mA_i)^{-1}\right),
\end{equation}
restricted to the observed components as in \eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag}, so the
conditional update of $(T_t, M_t)$ is identical to a single-tree
regression on the partial-residual data $\{(\vx_i, \va_i, \vr_i^{(t)})\}_{i=1}^n$.

For the rest of the chapter we fix $t$ and drop the superscript:
$\vr_i = \vr_i^{(t)}$, $T = T_t$, $M = M_t$. For each leaf $\ell \in L(T)$
define the index set and count
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-Iell}
	I_\ell = \{  i : \ell(\vx_i; T) = \ell  \},
	\qquad
	n_\ell = |I_\ell|.
\end{equation}
By construction $\sum_{\ell} n_\ell = n$ and the index sets $\{I_\ell\}$
partition $\{1,\dots,n\}$.

The per-datapoint precision matrix factors as a Hadamard product with an
outer product. Define
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-Atilde}
	\tilde\mA_i  \coloneqq  \va_i\va_i^\top  \in \R^{k\times k};
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-Pi}
	\mP_i  \coloneqq  \mA_i \mLam \mA_i
	= \mLam \odot \tilde\mA_i
	 \in \R^{k\times k},
\end{equation}
with $\odot$ the Hadamard (entrywise) product, since
$[\mA_i\mLam\mA_i]_{ab} = a_{i,a}\Lambda_{ab}a_{i,b}
	= \Lambda_{ab} [\tilde\mA_i]_{ab}$.

Because the per-datapoint precision varies with $i$, the per-leaf sufficient
statistics are not scalars but $k\times k$ matrices. Define the
$\mLam$-dependent posterior precision contribution and score by
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:mv-precision-sum}
	\mP_\ell & \coloneqq \sum_{i \in I_\ell}\mP_i
	= \sum_{i \in I_\ell}\mLam \odot \tilde\mA_i
	= \mLam \odot \mN_\ell
	 \in \R^{k\times k},
	\\
	\label{eq:mv-suffstats-B}
	\vB_\ell & \coloneqq \sum_{i \in I_\ell}\mP_i \vr_i
	= (\mLam \odot S_\ell) \Ind_k
	 \in \R^{k},
\end{align}
where $\Ind_k$ is the all-ones $k$-vector and the data-only sufficient
statistics are
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:mv-suffstats-N}
	\mN_\ell & \coloneqq \sum_{i \in I_\ell}\tilde\mA_i
	 \in \R^{k\times k},
	\\
	\label{eq:mv-suffstats-S}
	S_\ell   & \coloneqq \sum_{i \in I_\ell}\tilde\mA_i \odot (\Ind_k \vr_i^\top)
	 \in \R^{k\times k}.
\end{align}
Both $\mN_\ell, S_\ell$ depend only on the data (not on $\mLam$) and
accumulate additively across any partition of the index set. $\mN_\ell$ is
symmetric; $S_\ell$ in general is not. $\mP_\ell$ and $\vB_\ell$ depend on
$\mLam$ only and are computable from the $O(k^2)$ data-only statistics
$(\mN_\ell, S_\ell)$.

In the homoskedastic special case $\va_i \equiv \Ind_k$,
$\mN_\ell = n_\ell \Ind_k\Ind_k^\top$, the $a$-th row of $S_\ell$ equals
$\sum_{i \in I_\ell}\vr_i^\top$, $\mP_\ell = n_\ell \mLam$, and
$\vB_\ell = \mLam\sum_{i \in I_\ell}\vr_i$.

\section{Full conditional of the leaves}
\label{sec:mv-full-cond}

Conditional on $T$ and $\mLam$, the $n_\ell$ residuals in leaf $\ell$ are
independent Gaussian,
$\vr_{i,O_i} \sim \N_{k_i}(\vmu_{\ell,O_i}, [\mP_i]_{O_iO_i}^{-1})$ by
\eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag}; since the missing rows and columns of $\mP_i$
vanish, the log density is
$-\frac{1}{2}(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)^\top\mP_i(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)$ up to a
constant, as if $\vr_i$ were a complete $k$-vector with mean $\vmu_\ell$ and
precision $\mP_i$. Across leaves the residuals are independent because
$\{I_\ell\}$ partitions the data. Combined with $\vmu_\ell \sim \N_k(\mathbf{0}, \mLamm^{-1})$, this is a
Gaussian--Gaussian conjugate update with heterogeneous data precisions.

\begin{prop}[Leaf full conditional]
	\label{prop:mv-leaf}
	For each $\ell \in L(T)$,
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:mv-leaf-fc}
		\vmu_\ell \mid T, \mLam, \vr_{1:n} \sim
		\N_k\left(
		\underbrace{\left(\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right)^{-1} \vB_\ell}_{= \hat\vmu_\ell},
		\underbrace{\left(\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right)^{-1}}_{= V_\ell}
		\right),
	\end{equation}
	independently across $\ell$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
	The log of the joint of the data in leaf $\ell$ and the leaf prior, as a
	function of $\vmu_\ell$, is
	\begin{align}
		 & \sum_{i\in I_\ell}\log p(\vr_i \mid \vmu_\ell, \mLam)
		+ \log p(\vmu_\ell \mid \mLamm)                          \\
		 & = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_\ell}
		(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)^\top \mP_i (\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)
		-\frac{1}{2}\vmu_\ell^\top \mLamm  \vmu_\ell + \text{const.}
	\end{align}
	Expanding the quadratic form in $\vmu_\ell$,
	\begin{equation}
		(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)^\top \mP_i (\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)
		= \vr_i^\top \mP_i \vr_i - 2 \vmu_\ell^\top \mP_i \vr_i
		+ \vmu_\ell^\top \mP_i \vmu_\ell,
	\end{equation}
	and summing over $i \in I_\ell$, the $\vmu_\ell$-dependent terms collect,
	using \eqref{eq:mv-precision-sum}--\eqref{eq:mv-suffstats-B}, as
	\begin{equation}
		-\frac{1}{2}\vmu_\ell^\top
		\left(\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right) \vmu_\ell
		+ \vmu_\ell^\top \vB_\ell.
	\end{equation}
	Completing the square with $V_\ell^{-1} = \mLamm + \mP_\ell$ gives
	\begin{equation}
		-\frac{1}{2}(\vmu_\ell - \hat\vmu_\ell)^\top V_\ell^{-1}
		(\vmu_\ell - \hat\vmu_\ell) + \text{const},
		\qquad \hat\vmu_\ell = V_\ell \vB_\ell,
	\end{equation}
	yielding \eqref{eq:mv-leaf-fc}. Independence across leaves is immediate
	from the partition structure of $\{I_\ell\}$ and the independent leaf
	priors.
\end{proof}

In bartz, \eqref{eq:mv-leaf-fc} is computed by
\texttt{\_precompute\_leaf\_terms\_mv\_het} in \texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py}:
$\mN_\ell$ is accumulated in \texttt{Forest.prec\_tree} as the per-leaf sum
of \texttt{State.prec\_scale} $= \tilde\mA_i$, the Cholesky factor of
$\mLamm + \mLam \odot \mN_\ell$ is stored in \texttt{mean\_factor}, and
$\vB_\ell$ is formed by \texttt{compute\_B} from the per-leaf sum of
$\tilde\mA_i \odot (\Ind_k\vr_i^\top)$ returned by \texttt{sum\_resid}. When
$\va_i \equiv \Ind_k$ (or $\va_i = a_i\Ind_k$ with a scalar scale per
datapoint), $\mP_\ell = n_\ell \mLam$ (resp.\ $\sum_{i\in I_\ell} a_i^2 \mLam$)
and the cheaper \texttt{\_precompute\_leaf\_terms\_mv} is used instead, with
the count from \texttt{Forest.count\_tree}.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Marginal density with leaves integrated out}
\label{sec:mv-marg-lik}

Because the leaves are conjugate, we can integrate $M$ out analytically to
obtain a collapsed target on tree structure alone. Stack the residuals
belonging to leaf $\ell$ row-by-row into the matrix
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-Rell}
	\mR_\ell \coloneqq \{ \vr_i^\top : i \in I_\ell \} \in \R^{n_\ell \times k}.
\end{equation}
Conditional on $T$ and $\mLam$, the blocks $\mR_\ell$ are independent across
$\ell$ (the leaves partition the data), so the marginal density of the whole
residual set factorises over leaves.

\begin{prop}[Per-leaf marginal density]
	\label{prop:mv-marglik}
	For each leaf $\ell$,
	\begin{align}
		\log p(\mR_\ell \mid T, \mLam)
		 & = -\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}\sum_{i \in I_\ell} k_i
		+ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \in I_\ell}\log\left|[\mA_i\mLam\mA_i]_{O_iO_i}\right|
		\label{eq:mv-marglik-a}            \\
		 & \quad
		+ \frac{1}{2}\log|\mLamm|
		- \frac{1}{2}\log\left|\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right|
		\label{eq:mv-marglik-b}            \\
		 & \quad
		- \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \in I_\ell} \vr_i^\top \mP_i \vr_i
		+ \frac{1}{2} \vB_\ell^\top \left(\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right)^{-1}
		\vB_\ell,
		\label{eq:mv-marglik-c}
	\end{align}
	and the joint log marginal of the tree is
	$\log p(\mR_{1:|L(T)|} \mid T, \mLam) = \sum_{\ell \in L(T)}
		\log p(\mR_\ell \mid T, \mLam)$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
	By marginalisation over $\vmu_\ell$,
	\begin{equation}
		p(\mR_\ell \mid T, \mLam)
		= \int_{\R^k} p(\mR_\ell \mid \vmu_\ell, \mLam)
		p(\vmu_\ell \mid \mLamm)  d\vmu_\ell.
	\end{equation}
	Substituting the explicit Gaussian densities (the data factor in the
	precision form \eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag}; its quadratic form can be taken
	over all $k$ components because the missing rows and columns of $\mP_i$
	vanish),
	\begin{align}
		p(\mR_\ell \mid \vmu_\ell, \mLam)
		 & = \prod_{i \in I_\ell}(2\pi)^{-k_i/2}
		\left|[\mA_i\mLam\mA_i]_{O_iO_i}\right|^{1/2}
		\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)^\top\mP_i(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)\right), \\
		p(\vmu_\ell \mid \mLamm)
		 & = (2\pi)^{-k/2}|\mLamm|^{1/2}
		\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\vmu_\ell^\top\mLamm\vmu_\ell\right).
	\end{align}
	Expanding $(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)^\top\mP_i(\vr_i - \vmu_\ell)
		= \vr_i^\top\mP_i\vr_i - 2\vmu_\ell^\top\mP_i\vr_i
		+ \vmu_\ell^\top\mP_i\vmu_\ell$, summing over $i \in I_\ell$, and using
	\eqref{eq:mv-precision-sum}--\eqref{eq:mv-suffstats-B}, the joint exponent
	becomes
	\begin{equation}
		-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_\ell}\vr_i^\top\mP_i\vr_i
		+ \vmu_\ell^\top\vB_\ell
		- \frac{1}{2}\vmu_\ell^\top\left(\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right)\vmu_\ell.
	\end{equation}
	With $V_\ell^{-1} = \mLamm + \mP_\ell$ and
	$\hat\vmu_\ell = V_\ell\vB_\ell$, completing the square in $\vmu_\ell$
	splits the last two terms as
	\begin{equation}
		-\frac{1}{2}(\vmu_\ell - \hat\vmu_\ell)^\top V_\ell^{-1}
		(\vmu_\ell - \hat\vmu_\ell) + \frac{1}{2}\vB_\ell^\top V_\ell\vB_\ell,
	\end{equation}
	and the Gaussian integral over $\R^k$ evaluates to
	\begin{align}
		\int_{\R^k}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\vmu_\ell - \hat\vmu_\ell)^\top
		V_\ell^{-1}(\vmu_\ell - \hat\vmu_\ell)\right) d\vmu_\ell
		 & = (2\pi)^{k/2}|V_\ell|^{1/2}                        \\
		 & = (2\pi)^{k/2}\left|\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right|^{-1/2}.
	\end{align}
	Collecting all prefactors,
	\begin{align}
		p(\mR_\ell \mid T, \mLam)
		 & = \prod_{i\in I_\ell}\left[(2\pi)^{-k_i/2}
		\left|[\mA_i\mLam\mA_i]_{O_iO_i}\right|^{1/2}\right]
		\cdot (2\pi)^{-k/2}|\mLamm|^{1/2}                                           \\
		 & \quad \cdot (2\pi)^{k/2} \left|\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right|^{-1/2}           \\
		 & \quad \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_\ell}
		\vr_i^\top\mP_i\vr_i + \frac{1}{2}\vB_\ell^\top V_\ell\vB_\ell\right).
	\end{align}
	The $(2\pi)$ factors combine to $(2\pi)^{-\sum_{i\in I_\ell}k_i/2}$;
	taking the logarithm yields
	\eqref{eq:mv-marglik-a}--\eqref{eq:mv-marglik-c}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reduced form for tree-structure moves.}
Equation \eqref{eq:mv-marglik-a} and the term
$-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_\ell}\vr_i^\top\mP_i\vr_i$ are \emph{additive} in
the index set $I_\ell$: their sum across the leaves of any partition of a
fixed index set is the same. In particular, when comparing a leaf $\eta$
against its split $\eta \to (\eta_L, \eta_R)$ with $I_{\eta_L} \sqcup
	I_{\eta_R} = I_\eta$, those terms cancel exactly, and only the
log-determinant and quadratic-form pieces remain:
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:mv-reduced-marglik}
	\log p(\mR_\ell \mid T, \mLam) & =
	\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\log|\mLamm|
		- \frac{1}{2}\log\left|\mLamm + \mP_\ell\right|}_{\text{log-det term}}
	\\
	 & \quad + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\vB_\ell^\top\left(\mLamm + \mP_\ell
		\right)^{-1}\vB_\ell}_{\text{quadratic term}}
	+ (\text{cancels in splits / prunes}).
	\notag
\end{align}
In bartz these two terms are assembled in \texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py} by
\texttt{\_precompute\_likelihood\_terms\_mv\_het} for general $\va_i$, and by
\texttt{\_precompute\_likelihood\_terms\_mv} when $\va_i \equiv \Ind_k$ or
the scales are scalar per datapoint, so that $\mP_\ell \propto \mLam$. Note
that the
data-only statistics $\mN_\ell$ and $S_\ell$ in
\eqref{eq:mv-suffstats-N}--\eqref{eq:mv-suffstats-S} are additive across
partitions, so the relevant cancellations in the GROW/PRUNE comparisons
involve only the six quantities
$(\mN_{\eta_L}, \mN_{\eta_R}, \mN_\eta, S_{\eta_L}, S_{\eta_R}, S_\eta)$ with
$\mN_\eta = \mN_{\eta_L} + \mN_{\eta_R}$ and
$S_\eta = S_{\eta_L} + S_{\eta_R}$ (and likewise for the derived
$\vB_\eta = \vB_{\eta_L} + \vB_{\eta_R}$).

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Acceptance ratios for GROW and PRUNE}
\label{sec:mv-lkratios}

The MCMC sampler updates each tree by proposing a structural move on $T$
\emph{after marginalising over $M$}: the marginal posterior of $T$
conditional on $\mLam$ and the partial residuals is the target. This is the
standard ``integrate-the-leaves'' trick; sampling $M$ is then deferred to
the post-acceptance step using Proposition~\ref{prop:mv-leaf}.

\subsection{The GROW move}
\label{sec:mv-grow}

Pick a leaf $\eta \in L(T)$ and replace it by an internal node with a fresh
splitting rule and two leaf children $\eta_L, \eta_R$. Call the new tree $T'$.
The Metropolis--Hastings acceptance ratio for this move, with the leaves of
both $T$ and $T'$ marginalised, is
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-mh}
	\mathrm{MH}_{\mathrm{grow}}
	= \underbrace{\frac{q(T \mid T')}{q(T' \mid T)}}_{\text{transition}}
	\cdot \underbrace{\frac{p(T')}{p(T)}}_{\text{prior}}
	\cdot \underbrace{\frac{p(\vr_{1:n} \mid T', \mLam)}{p(\vr_{1:n} \mid T, \mLam)}}_{\text{marginal density}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Marginal density ratio.}
Only the leaf $\eta$ (in $T$) and the leaves $\eta_L, \eta_R$ (in $T'$)
differ between the two collapsed densities; every other leaf has identical
sufficient statistics in $T$ and $T'$. Using the reduced form
\eqref{eq:mv-reduced-marglik},
\begin{align}
	\log\frac{p(\vr_{1:n} \mid T', \mLam)}{p(\vr_{1:n} \mid T, \mLam)}
	 & = \frac{1}{2}\log|\mLamm|
	+ \frac{1}{2}\log\left|\mLamm + \mP_\eta\right|
	- \frac{1}{2}\log\left|\mLamm + \mP_{\eta_L}\right|
	- \frac{1}{2}\log\left|\mLamm + \mP_{\eta_R}\right|
	\notag \\
	 & \quad + \frac{1}{2}\vB_{\eta_L}^\top\left(\mLamm + \mP_{\eta_L}\right)^{-1}\vB_{\eta_L}
	+ \frac{1}{2}\vB_{\eta_R}^\top\left(\mLamm + \mP_{\eta_R}\right)^{-1}\vB_{\eta_R}
	\notag \\
	 & \quad - \frac{1}{2}\vB_\eta^\top\left(\mLamm + \mP_\eta\right)^{-1}\vB_\eta,
	\label{eq:mv-lkratio-grow}
\end{align}
with $\mN_\eta = \mN_{\eta_L} + \mN_{\eta_R}$, $S_\eta = S_{\eta_L} + S_{\eta_R}$,
and hence $\mP_\eta = \mP_{\eta_L} + \mP_{\eta_R}$ and
$\vB_\eta = \vB_{\eta_L} + \vB_{\eta_R}$ by the additivity of
\eqref{eq:mv-suffstats-N}--\eqref{eq:mv-suffstats-S}. The four
log-determinants and three quadratic forms can be computed stably from
Cholesky factors of $\mLamm + \mP_*$. This is what bartz does:
\texttt{\_precompute\_likelihood\_terms\_mv\_het} gathers the factors and
their log-determinants at $\eta_L$, $\eta_R$, $\eta$, and
\texttt{\_compute\_likelihood\_ratio\_mv\_het} evaluates the quadratic forms
by triangular solves against $\vB_*$. When $\va_i \equiv \Ind_k$ the
statistics $\mN_*$ and $S_*$ collapse to scaled counts and sums, and
\texttt{\_precompute\_likelihood\_terms\_mv} /
\texttt{\_compute\_likelihood\_ratio\_mv} instead precompute the sandwich
$\mLam(\mLamm + \mP_*)^{-1}\mLam$ and contract it with the plain
residual sums.

\paragraph{Prior ratio.}
Let $d_\eta$ be the depth of $\eta$, so that $\eta_L, \eta_R$ are at depth
$d_\eta + 1$. The only nodes whose prior status changes between $T$ and
$T'$ are $\eta$ (leaf $\to$ internal) and $\eta_L, \eta_R$ (introduced as
leaves). Writing $p_d \coloneqq p_{\mathrm{NT}}(d)$ for brevity,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-prior-ratio-grow}
	\frac{p(T')}{p(T)}
	= \underbrace{\frac{p_{d_\eta}}{1 - p_{d_\eta}}}_{\eta \text{ now internal}}
	\cdot \underbrace{(1 - p_{d_\eta + 1})^{2}}_{\eta_L,\eta_R \text{ are leaves}}
	\cdot \underbrace{\frac{s_v}{e_\eta} \cdot \frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{cut}}(\eta, v)}}_{\text{rule prior at } \eta},
\end{equation}
where $s_v / e_\eta$ is the variable prior \eqref{eq:mv-var-prior} at
$\eta$ and $n_{\mathrm{cut}}(\eta, v)$ is the number of valid cutpoints for
the chosen variable $v$. (If a child $\eta_L$ or
$\eta_R$ would have no further admissible split, its $1 - p_{d_\eta+1}$
factor is replaced by $1$, since the prior assigns it leaf status with
probability $1$. In the bartz code, this is encoded by the children
entries of \texttt{Moves.lrt\_growable}, used in \texttt{complete\_ratio}.)

\paragraph{Transition ratio.}
Let $G(T)$ be the set of \emph{growable} leaves of $T$: those with
$E(\eta) \ne \emptyset$ and $d_\eta < d_{\max}$. The forward kernel at $T$
chooses GROW with probability $1/2$ (or $1$ if PRUNE is impossible, i.e.,
$T$ is the root), then picks $\eta \in G(T)$ with probability proportional
to a fixed positive weight $w_\eta$ attached to each node position, then a
variable from the eligible ones with the prior probabilities $s_v / e_\eta$
of \eqref{eq:mv-var-prior}, then a cutpoint uniformly from the
$n_{\mathrm{cut}}(\eta, v)$ admissible ones:
\[
	q(T' \mid T) = \frac{1}{2(\text{or }1)} \cdot
	\frac{w_\eta}{W(T)} \cdot
	\frac{s_v}{e_\eta} \cdot
	\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{cut}}(\eta, v)},
	\qquad
	W(T) \coloneqq \sum_{\nu \in G(T)} w_\nu.
\]
CGM take $w \equiv 1$, a uniform choice among the growable leaves; bartz
keeps $w$ in \texttt{Forest.p\_propose\_grow} and sets $w_\eta = p_{d_\eta}$
by default, favouring shallow leaves. The reverse kernel at $T'$ chooses
PRUNE with probability $1/2$ (or $1$ if $T'$ has no growable leaves left),
then picks the prunable internal node uniformly from the $|P(T')|$
candidates ($P(T')$ being the set of internal nodes whose two children are
both leaves):
\[
	q(T \mid T') = \frac{1}{2(\text{or }1)} \cdot \frac{1}{|P(T')|}.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-trans-ratio-grow}
	\frac{q(T \mid T')}{q(T' \mid T)}
	= \frac{P_{\mathrm{prune}}(T')}{P_{\mathrm{grow}}(T)} \cdot
	\frac{W(T)}{w_\eta\,|P(T')|} \cdot
	\frac{e_\eta}{s_v} \cdot n_{\mathrm{cut}}(\eta, v),
\end{equation}
with $P_{\mathrm{grow}}, P_{\mathrm{prune}} \in \{\frac{1}{2}, 1\}$ as just
described. In bartz the leaf is drawn by \texttt{choose\_leaf} in
\texttt{mcmcstep/\_moves.py}, which returns $w_\eta / W(T)$ as
\texttt{prob\_choose}, and the variable by \texttt{choose\_variable}, a
categorical draw on \texttt{Forest.log\_s} with the ineligible variables
masked out (\texttt{categorical\_exclude}).

\paragraph{Combined.}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:mv-prior-ratio-grow} and
\eqref{eq:mv-trans-ratio-grow}, the rule factors $s_v / e_\eta$ and
$1/n_{\mathrm{cut}}(\eta, v)$ cancel, since the proposal draws the rule from
its prior:
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-nonlk-grow}
	\boxed{
		\frac{q(T \mid T') p(T')}{q(T' \mid T) p(T)}
		= \frac{P_{\mathrm{prune}}(T')}{P_{\mathrm{grow}}(T)} \cdot
		\frac{W(T)}{w_\eta\,|P(T')|} \cdot
		\frac{p_{d_\eta}}{1 - p_{d_\eta}} \cdot
		(1 - p_{d_\eta+1})^{2}.
	}
\end{equation}
This non-likelihood factor depends only on the tree topology, the CGM prior,
and the leaf weights, not on the leaf or noise model nor on $\vs$. With
$w \equiv 1$ it is the standard CGM form, $W(T) / w_\eta = |G(T)|$; with the
bartz default $w_\eta = p_{d_\eta}$ the $p_{d_\eta}$ cancels. It is precisely
\texttt{Moves.log\_trans\_prior\_ratio}: \texttt{compute\_partial\_ratio} in
\texttt{mcmcstep/\_moves.py} computes
$1 / (P_{\mathrm{grow}}(T)\,\texttt{prob\_choose}\,|P(T')|)$ (the variable /
cutpoint cancellation is the comment ``the transition and prior ratios also
contain factors num\_available\_split * num\_available\_var * s[var], but
they cancel out''), and \texttt{complete\_ratio} in
\texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py} multiplies in $P_{\mathrm{prune}}(T')$ and the
two prior factors. The count $|G(T)|$ (\texttt{Moves.num\_growable}) enters
only through $P_{\mathrm{prune}}(T')$, which is $1/2$ iff $T'$ has a growable
leaf.

\subsection{The PRUNE move}
\label{sec:mv-prune}

A PRUNE move acts as the inverse of a GROW: it picks an internal node
$\eta \in P(T)$ whose two children are leaves, deletes the children, and
turns $\eta$ into a leaf. Because the two moves form a reversible pair, the
acceptance ratio of a PRUNE from $T$ to $T'$ is simply the reciprocal of the
GROW ratio for the move $T' \to T$:
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-mh-prune}
	\mathrm{MH}_{\mathrm{prune}}
	= \mathrm{MH}_{\mathrm{grow}}^{ -1}(T' \to T).
\end{equation}
In particular, the log marginal-density ratio is the negative of
\eqref{eq:mv-lkratio-grow}, with the sufficient statistics
$(\mN_{\eta_L}, \mN_{\eta_R}, S_{\eta_L}, S_{\eta_R})$ read off the
to-be-deleted children, and the non-likelihood factor
\eqref{eq:mv-nonlk-grow} inverted for the inverse move. Both \texttt{bartz} and \texttt{stochtree}
exploit this symmetry:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \texttt{bartz} evaluates the GROW ratio for both moves and negates
	      the log-ratio when the proposal is PRUNE
	      (\texttt{accept\_move\_and\_sample\_leaves} in
	      \texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py}: \texttt{log\_ratio = jnp.where(pt.move.grow,
		      log\_ratio, -log\_ratio)}).
	\item \texttt{stochtree} compares
	      \texttt{NoSplitLogMarginalLikelihood} against
	      \texttt{SplitLogMarginalLikelihood} with sign reversed for prune
	      steps inside \texttt{sampler.cpp}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sampling the leaves after acceptance}
\label{sec:mv-sample-leaves}

Whenever the structure update is finalised (whether the proposal was
accepted or rejected), the leaves of $T$ are resampled from
\eqref{eq:mv-leaf-fc}. In the sum-of-trees setting, the residuals of every
other tree are then refreshed by subtracting the new
$g(\cdot;  T_t, M_t)$ from the running sum, and the cycle moves to the
next tree. After all $m$ trees have been updated, the latent outcomes,
$\mLam$, $\vs$ and $\theta$ are drawn from their full conditionals,
derived next.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Updating the latent outcomes}
\label{sec:mv-z}

After all $m$ trees have been updated, the latent outcomes of the observed
binary components are drawn from their full conditional. Under the diagonal
form of $\mLam$ with $\lambda_j = 1$ for $j \in \setB$, the components of
$\vyt_i$ in \eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag} are independent given the trees, so
$z_{i,j}$ depends on the rest of the model only through $f_j(\vx_i)$,
$a_{i,j}$ and $y_{i,j}$.

\begin{prop}[Latent outcome full conditional]
	\label{prop:mv-z}
	For each $j \in \setB$ and each $i$ with $a_{i,j} > 0$,
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:mv-z-fc}
		z_{i,j} \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, y_{i,j} \sim
		\N\left(f_j(\vx_i), a_{i,j}^{-2}\right)
		\text{ truncated to }
		\begin{cases}
			(0, \infty)   & y_{i,j} = 1, \\
			(-\infty, 0] & y_{i,j} = 0,
		\end{cases}
	\end{equation}
	independently across $i$ and $j$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
	By \eqref{eq:mv-joint}, the factors involving $z_{i,j}$ are
	$p(\vyt_{i,O_i} \mid T_{1:m}, M_{1:m}, \mLam)$ and
	$p(y_{i,j} \mid z_{i,j})$. Since $\mLam$ is diagonal, the former
	factorises over the components of $\vyt_{i,O_i}$, and its $j$-th factor is
	the density of $\N(f_j(\vx_i), (a_{i,j}^2 \lambda_j)^{-1})$ at $z_{i,j}$,
	with $\lambda_j = 1$. Multiplying by the indicator
	$\Ind[y_{i,j} = \Ind[z_{i,j} > 0]]$ restricts it to the half-line
	\eqref{eq:mv-z-fc}. The factorisation over $i$ and $j$ gives the
	independence.
\end{proof}

In standardised units, $a_{i,j}(z_{i,j} - f_j(\vx_i))$ is a standard normal
truncated below at $-a_{i,j} f_j(\vx_i)$ if $y_{i,j} = 1$, and above it
otherwise. This is how \texttt{step\_z} in \texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py}
draws it: it forms the threshold from the trees and from $a_{i,j}$, stored in
\texttt{State.inv\_sdev\_scale}, draws the standardised variate with
\texttt{truncated\_normal\_onesided}, and multiplies it by
\texttt{State.error\_scale} $= 1/a_{i,j}$. Since the trees are held fixed,
the result is stored directly as the new residual $z_{i,j} - f_j(\vx_i)$ of
component $j$. \texttt{State.z} stores only the binary rows of $\vyt_i$,
selected by \texttt{State.binary\_indices}; the latent outcomes of missing
binary components are drawn and ignored, which is harmless because every
consumer of the residuals multiplies them by $a_{i,j} = 0$.

\section{Updating the error precision}
\label{sec:mv-Sigma}

After all $m$ trees and the latent outcomes have been updated, the global
precision $\mLam$ is drawn from its full conditional. Let
$\bm{\veps}_i \coloneqq \vyt_i - f(\vx_i)$ be the residual of the full
forest, and let
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-eps-tilde}
	\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i \coloneqq \mA_i \bm{\veps}_i
	= \va_i \odot \bm{\veps}_i
\end{equation}
be its rescaling, which vanishes on the missing components. By
\eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag},
$\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_{i,O_i} \mid \mLam \sim \N_{k_i}(\mathbf{0}, \mLam_{O_iO_i}^{-1})$
independently across $i$. Write
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-Sigma-rate}
	\Psi' \coloneqq \Psi + \sum_{i=1}^n \tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i \tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i^\top
	= \Psi + \sum_{i=1}^n \tilde\mA_i \odot (\bm{\veps}_i \bm{\veps}_i^\top)
\end{equation}
for the posterior rate matrix of the dense form, and
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-nj}
	n_j \coloneqq |\{i : j \in O_i\}|
\end{equation}
for the number of observed values of component $j$.

\begin{prop}[Error precision full conditional]
	\label{prop:mv-Sigma}
	\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
		\item Under the dense prior \eqref{eq:mv-iw-prior} (so that
		      $O_i = \{1,\dots,k\}$ for all $i$),
		      \begin{equation}
			      \label{eq:mv-Sigma-fc}
			      \mLam \mid \cdots \sim \W\left(\nu + n, \Psi'\right).
		      \end{equation}
		\item Under the diagonal prior \eqref{eq:mv-gamma-prior}, the
		      components stay independent a posteriori,
		      \begin{equation}
			      \label{eq:mv-Sigma-fc-diag}
			      \lambda_j \mid \cdots \sim
			      \Gamma\left(\frac{\nu_j + n_j}{2},
			      \frac{\psi_j + \sum_{i=1}^n a_{i,j}^2 \veps_{i,j}^2}{2}\right),
			      \qquad j \in \setC,
		      \end{equation}
		      whatever the pattern of missing values, while $\lambda_j = 1$
		      for $j \in \setB$ stays fixed.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
	The joint log density of $\mLam$ and the observed rescaled residuals,
	treating $\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_{1:n}$ as observed, is
	\begin{align}
		\log p(\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_{1:n} \mid \mLam) + \log p(\mLam)
		 & = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[
		\frac{1}{2}\log\left|\mLam_{O_iO_i}\right|
		- \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i^\top \mLam \tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i
		\right] + \log p(\mLam) + \text{const},
	\end{align}
	where the quadratic form is taken over all $k$ components because
	$\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i$ vanishes off $O_i$. Writing
	$\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i^\top \mLam \tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i =
		\tr(\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i\tilde{\bm{\veps}}_i^\top \mLam)$ and collecting
	the trace terms into $\Psi'$, this is
	\begin{equation}
		\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n \log\left|\mLam_{O_iO_i}\right|
		- \frac{1}{2}\tr(\Psi' \mLam) + \frac{1}{2}\tr(\Psi \mLam)
		+ \log p(\mLam) + \text{const}.
	\end{equation}
	For (i), $\log p(\mLam) = \frac{\nu - k - 1}{2}\log|\mLam| -
		\frac{1}{2}\tr(\Psi \mLam) + \text{const}$ and
	$|\mLam_{O_iO_i}| = |\mLam|$ for every $i$, so the exponent of
	$|\mLam|$ is $(\nu + n - k - 1)/2$ and the density is the kernel of
	$\W(\nu + n, \Psi')$.
	For (ii), $\log p(\mLam) = \sum_{j \in \setC} [(\nu_j/2 - 1)\log\lambda_j
			- \psi_j\lambda_j/2] + \text{const}$ on the support
	$\lambda_j = 1$, $j \in \setB$;
	$|\mLam_{O_iO_i}| = \prod_{j \in O_i}\lambda_j$, and
	$\tr((\Psi' - \Psi)\mLam) = \sum_j \lambda_j \sum_i a_{i,j}^2
		\veps_{i,j}^2$, so the density factorises over $j \in \setC$ as
	$\prod_j \lambda_j^{(\nu_j + n_j)/2 - 1}
		\exp(-(\psi_j + \sum_i a_{i,j}^2\veps_{i,j}^2)\lambda_j/2)$.
\end{proof}

The proof also shows why the diagonal restriction of \S\ref{sec:mv-model}
is needed with missing values: $\prod_i |\mLam_{O_iO_i}|^{1/2}$ is not a
power of $|\mLam|$, so even the likelihood \eqref{eq:mv-lik-diag} would not
be conjugate to the Wishart prior for a dense $\mLam$, and the actual
marginal likelihood \eqref{eq:mv-lik-obs} involves the Schur complements
$[(\mLam^{-1})_{O_iO_i}]^{-1}$ on top of that. Whole missing datapoints
contribute nothing to either the sum of log determinants or $\Psi'$, so (i)
holds with $n$ the number of present datapoints; this is the case bartz
keeps dense, with \texttt{df\_post} $= \nu + n$ computed in
\texttt{\_step\_error\_cov\_inv\_mv} from \texttt{State.n\_non\_missing}.
For $k = 1$, (i) and (ii)
coincide: $\lambda = 1/\sigsq$ has a $\mathrm{Gamma}(\nu/2, \psi/2)$ prior,
i.e.\ $\sigsq \sim \IG(\nu/2, \psi/2)$, and the update is
$\sigsq \mid \cdots \sim \IG\bigl((\nu + n)/2, (\psi + \sum_i a_i^2
	\veps_i^2)/2\bigr)$, with $n$ replaced by the number of observed
outcomes.

Note that the heteroskedasticity is handled \emph{exactly} by the residual
rescaling $\bm{\veps}_i \mapsto \va_i \odot \bm{\veps}_i$: no auxiliary
sampler or block update is required.

\paragraph{Sampling.} A draw from $\W(\nu', \Psi')$ is obtained directly,
e.g.\ by a Bartlett decomposition of the Cholesky factor of $(\Psi')^{-1}$;
this is what \texttt{\_sample\_wishart\_bartlett} and
\texttt{\_step\_error\_cov\_inv\_mv} do in bartz (\texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py}),
the latter rescaling the residuals by \texttt{State.inv\_sdev\_scale}
$= \va_i$ before forming $\Psi' = \Psi + \tilde R^\top \tilde R$, with
$\tilde R$ the row-rescaled residual matrix. The diagonal update
\eqref{eq:mv-Sigma-fc-diag} is \texttt{\_step\_error\_cov\_inv\_diag}, with
the prior represented by \texttt{DiagWishart}, whose \texttt{nu} and
\texttt{rate} map to $\nu_j$ and $\psi_j$ through
\eqref{eq:mv-gamma-match}; a zero diagonal entry of \texttt{rate} marks a
fixed component, which the update resets to $1$.

\section{Updating the variable selection probabilities}
\label{sec:mv-sparse}

Let $B$ be the set of internal nodes across all $m$ trees, $v_\eta$ the
split variable of $\eta \in B$, and
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-varcount}
	c_v \coloneqq |\{\eta \in B : v_\eta = v\}|
\end{equation}
the number of times variable $v$ is used in the forest. The trees enter the
full conditional of $\vs$ only through the variable factors
\eqref{eq:mv-var-prior} of their rules, since the cutpoint factors do not
involve $\vs$. By \eqref{eq:mv-joint} and \eqref{eq:mv-s-prior},
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-s-fc}
	p(\vs \mid T_{1:m}, \theta)
	\propto \prod_{v=1}^p s_v^{\theta/p - 1}
	\prod_{\eta \in B} \frac{s_{v_\eta}}{e_\eta}
	= \prod_{v=1}^p s_v^{\theta/p + c_v - 1}
	\prod_{\eta \in B} e_\eta^{-1}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Approximate update.}
If every variable were eligible at every node, $e_\eta \equiv 1$ and
\eqref{eq:mv-s-fc} would be the kernel of
$\Dir(\theta/p + c_1, \dots, \theta/p + c_p)$: the Dirichlet is conjugate
to the categorical rule draws. This is exact
when no variable can be exhausted, as with continuous cutpoints; with binned
cutpoints it ignores the denominators, and bartz uses it as an approximation
when \texttt{augment} is off. The approximation is worst with few variables
with few cutpoints each, where ancestor splits often exhaust a variable.

\paragraph{Exact update by data augmentation.}
The denominators are removed by augmenting each internal node with an
exponential weight and each variable with a Poisson count.

\begin{prop}[Variable probabilities full conditional]
	\label{prop:mv-s}
	Conditional on $\vs$, draw independently
	\begin{align}
		\label{eq:mv-s-aug-w}
		w_\eta \mid \vs
		 & \sim \Expd(e_\eta),
		\qquad \eta \in B,
		\\
		\label{eq:mv-s-aug}
		\tilde c_v \mid \vs, w
		 & \sim \Pois(s_v  \omega_v),
		\qquad
		\omega_v \coloneqq \sum_{\eta \in B :  v \notin E(\eta)} w_\eta,
		\qquad v = 1, \dots, p,
	\end{align}
	with $\Expd(e_\eta)$ the exponential of rate $e_\eta$. Then
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:mv-s-fc-aug}
		\vs \mid T_{1:m}, \theta, \tilde c_{1:p} \sim
		\Dir\left(\frac{\theta}{p} + c_1 + \tilde c_1, \dots,
		\frac{\theta}{p} + c_p + \tilde c_p\right),
	\end{equation}
	and marginalising $(w, \tilde c)$ out of the joint of
	$(\vs, w, \tilde c)$ recovers \eqref{eq:mv-s-fc}.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
	For each $\eta \in B$, using $1 - e_\eta = \sum_{u \notin E(\eta)} s_u$,
	\begin{equation}
		\frac{1}{e_\eta}
		= \int_0^\infty \mathrm{d}w  e^{-w e_\eta}
		= \int_0^\infty \mathrm{d}w  e^{-w} \prod_{u \notin E(\eta)} e^{w s_u}
		= \int_0^\infty \mathrm{d}w  e^{-w} \prod_{u \notin E(\eta)}
		\sum_{c = 0}^\infty \frac{(w s_u)^c}{c!}.
	\end{equation}
	Substituting into \eqref{eq:mv-s-fc}, the integrand and summand define
	the joint density of $\vs$, the weights $w = (w_\eta)_{\eta \in B}$ and
	the per-node counts $c_{\eta,u}$, $u \notin E(\eta)$,
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:mv-s-aug-joint}
		p(\vs, w, c \mid T_{1:m}, \theta)
		\propto \prod_{v=1}^p s_v^{\theta/p + c_v - 1}
		\prod_{\eta \in B} e^{-w_\eta}
		\prod_{u \notin E(\eta)} \frac{(w_\eta s_u)^{c_{\eta,u}}}{c_{\eta,u}!},
	\end{equation}
	whose marginal over $(w, c)$ is \eqref{eq:mv-s-fc} by construction.
	Conditional on $\vs$, the nodes are independent; summing
	\eqref{eq:mv-s-aug-joint} over the counts of node $\eta$ gives
	$e^{-w_\eta} e^{w_\eta (1 - e_\eta)} = e^{-w_\eta e_\eta}$, i.e.\
	$w_\eta \sim \Expd(e_\eta)$, and conditional on $w_\eta$ the counts are
	independent $c_{\eta,u} \sim \Pois(w_\eta s_u)$. Collecting the powers of
	$s_v$ in \eqref{eq:mv-s-aug-joint}, only the totals
	$\tilde c_v = \sum_{\eta : v \notin E(\eta)} c_{\eta,v}$ matter, and
	they are $\Pois(s_v \omega_v)$ as sums of independent Poissons. Conditional
	on the counts, \eqref{eq:mv-s-aug-joint} is the kernel of
	\eqref{eq:mv-s-fc-aug}.
\end{proof}

The counts have a sampling interpretation: drawing $v$ from $\vs$ until it
lands in $E(\eta)$ is a rejection sampler for \eqref{eq:mv-var-prior}, and
$c_{\eta,u}$ is the number of rejected draws of $u$. This augmentation is
also how Linero (2018) accounts for the ineligible variables. At a single
node the counts are negative multinomial, but their sum across nodes has no
closed form, whence the exponential mixing that turns them into Poissons,
with a fixed amount of work per node and per variable.

In bartz, \texttt{sample\_s\_augmentation} in \texttt{mcmcstep/\_step.py}
draws $w_\eta$ as a standard exponential divided by $e_\eta$ and accumulates
$\omega_v$ tree by tree in \texttt{\_blocked\_mass\_tree}, using the fact
that a node can exhaust only its own split variable, and only at one node
along any root-to-leaf path, so that both $1 - e_\eta$ and $\omega_v$ are
level-by-level accumulations over the tree heap. \texttt{step\_s} adds the Poisson draws to $c_v$ from
\texttt{var\_histogram} and draws \eqref{eq:mv-s-fc-aug} as independent
log-gamma variates, stored unnormalised in \texttt{Forest.log\_s}.

\paragraph{Updating the concentration.}
Under \eqref{eq:mv-theta-prior}, $\theta = \rho \xi / (1 - \xi)$ and
$\theta$ depends on the rest of the model only through $\vs$. Multiplying the
Beta density of $\xi$ by \eqref{eq:mv-s-prior} as a function of $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mv-theta-fc}
	p(\xi \mid \vs) \propto
	\xi^{a_\theta - 1} (1 - \xi)^{b_\theta - 1}
	\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta/p)^p}
	\prod_{v=1}^p s_v^{\theta/p},
	\qquad \theta = \frac{\rho \xi}{1 - \xi},
\end{equation}
where the factor $\prod_v s_v^{-1}$ of the Dirichlet density has been
dropped as constant in $\theta$. No Jacobian appears because $\xi$ itself is
the sampled variable. This is not a standard density; \texttt{step\_theta}
samples $\xi$ from \eqref{eq:mv-theta-fc} discretised on the midpoints of
$1000$ equal bins of $(0, 1)$, then sets $\theta$.

\paragraph{Schedule.}
bartz enables these two updates only from step
\texttt{StepConfig.sparse\_on\_at} onwards (by default, half of the burn-in),
keeping $\vs$ and $\theta$ at their initial values before; by default the
initial $\vs$ is uniform, $s_v = 1/p$, so the early sampler draws the split
variable uniformly at random.
